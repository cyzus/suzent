Suzent Regent, the agent at the top of the sidebar

Talk to Regent to see every project at once, hand work to project sessions, and hear only about what needs you.

此页面目前提供英文内容，中文翻译尚未完成。

Suzent Regent, or Regent for short, sits above your chats in the sidebar. It is the one conversation that watches all of your projects: you tell it what you want done, it hands the work to a session in the right project, follows up while you are away, and tells you when something needs your decision.

Use it when you don't want to open each project to see where things stand, or when you want to say "get this done" and come back later.

How it differs from your other chats

The short version: chats in a project do the work; Regent keeps track of all of it for you.

Hand it work

Tell Regent what you want, and which project it belongs to if that isn't obvious. It either:

  • sends the work to a session already in that project, waking it up with your request, or
  • starts a new session in the project to take it on, with a task on the project's task list.

It can also hand the work to a coding agent you've set up under Settings → ACP Agents, such as Codex or Claude Code: say "have Codex fix this in the website project" and Regent starts a session in that project that runs on Codex.

It can also message Suzent on your other paired devices, for example to run something on your laptop. It sends the message and moves on; the reply comes back to it later.

Your request shows up at the start of the new session, so you can open it any time and see what was asked. The session then works on its own. When it finishes, or gets stuck on something only you can answer, it reports back to Regent, and Regent tells you.

caution

Sessions that Regent starts run in Smart mode: low-risk actions go ahead, and anything else is reviewed by the security model, which may still ask you. Your deny rules and the built-in safety checks apply as usual. To have a session ask before every change, open it and switch its mode to Ask. See Permission modes.

Sessions on a coding agent start in Ask mode instead, since there is no security model to review their actions. Open the session to approve what it asks; a request nobody answers is denied after a while, and the agent reports that back.

See what's going on

The Regent card in the sidebar shows, at a glance, how many chats are waiting on you and how many are running.

Projects and chats holding something that waits on you get a badge in the sidebar too.

Regent sits at the very top of the sidebar, above everything else, like a pinned contact; New chat and Search sit just above your projects. Open Regent and its row grows into a profile with Regent's picture, while the rest of the sidebar fades back until you point at it. The header shows Regent's name and a small tag for what needs you; with the sidebar collapsed, Regent's picture takes the logo's place in the header.

The board

The Tasks side panel on Regent's chat lists the chats in your projects by where they stand for you. Pick one project from the dropdown to narrow it down.

  • Waiting on you: the chat asked you something, wants a tool approved, has a task stuck on you, or its last reply leaves the next move to you.
  • Running: it's working right now.
  • Idle: nothing is happening and nothing needs you.
  • Resolved: you marked it done. This group starts folded.

Each row shows the project, what the chat needs from you when it waits, and a one-line note on where the work stands. Idle and resolved chats leave the board after a week without activity.

You decide when a chat is resolved: right-click it on the board and choose Mark resolved, or open it from the board and click ✓ next to its title in the header. Resolving doesn't touch the chat's tasks. If the chat starts working again or needs you again, it leaves Resolved by itself.

Suzent's Decision model (Settings → Model Roles) reads the end of each reply to tell whether the chat is waiting on you and to write the one-line note.

Regent's picture tells you its state at a glance, here and on the sidebar card:

  • Still: nothing needs you.
  • Eyes scanning: it's checking in on sessions or answering you.
  • Orange gem: something is waiting on your decision.
  • A small nod: it just said something new.

If your system is set to reduce motion, the picture stays still.

Open a session from the board and Suzent switches to it: the sidebar opens that session's project and briefly highlights it, and the header reads Regent / session title. Click Regent there to go back.

It checks in on its own

Regent has a heartbeat that is on from the start and runs every 30 minutes. A check-in looks for sessions that have stalled and for questions you haven't answered in a while. When everything is fine, it stays quiet and nothing is added to the chat. If it nudged a stalled session along the way, the check-in stays in the chat so Regent remembers it, but you aren't notified.

To change what it looks for, edit its heartbeat.md checklist from Regent's Config tab. You can also change how often it runs, or turn it off; turning it off sticks.

Reminders and follow-ups

Ask Regent to remind you of something, or to check back on some work later:

Remind me at 5pm to send the weekly update.

Check on the docs rewrite in an hour and tell me if it's stuck.

A reminder always tells you. A check only tells you if there is something worth saying. Both are listed under Scheduled wake-ups in Regent's Tools tab, where you can cancel them.

When Regent says something while you are elsewhere, its card shows the new message and you get a desktop notification.

Regent keeps its replies short and only brings you what needs you. When a session reports back or finishes and there is nothing for you to do, Regent notes it in the chat as Nothing needed from you, without a notification or an unread badge. Work that is blocked on you always notifies you.

What it remembers about working with you

Regent keeps its own short notes on how you want it to work: what to bring to you and what to handle itself, what counts as urgent, when not to disturb you, which project you mean by a nickname, and how you like your reports. Tell it a rule ("don't ping me about tests failing, just restart them") and it writes it down; it also notes rules you keep repeating.

The notes start with empty sections, Bring to the user, Handle without asking, Don't disturb, Project names, and Reports, and each rule goes under the one it belongs to. Regent reads them at the start of every turn. They never hold project or task status, which it looks up fresh each time; for what a project is about, it reads a line from that project's own context instead of keeping a copy. To read or change them yourself, open Regent's Context tab and click Edit.

What it learns about you in general goes to your memory, like any other chat.

What it won't do

Regent can read files and search the web, your memory, and past conversations, but apart from its own notes it can't edit files, run commands, or use MCP servers, and the tool picker doesn't apply to it. That keeps one agent with a view of every project from making changes in all of them at once: the work happens in a project session, where you can see and steer it.

Regent only knows about a project's sessions through their tasks and what's running. A chat in a project that never made a task and isn't running doesn't show up for it.

Regent on your phone

The iOS and Android apps show Regent above your other conversations when your phone has All conversations access. It is the same Regent conversation as on your computer, with the same history and project context. Opening Regent for the first time also sets up its usual check-ins.

The crowned mark moves while Regent works. An orange gem means something needs you, and a badge shows unread messages. Regent's separate replies and reactions appear in the conversation, alongside expandable activity and tool approvals. Animations pause in the background and respect reduced-motion settings. Expand Recent delegated conversations to jump to one of the latest project sessions Regent started.

Phones restricted to selected conversations cannot open Regent, because Regent can read and coordinate work across projects. Sending, stopping and approving still require their corresponding phone permissions. Your computer must be running and reachable. Background phone push notifications are not provided by this integration; return to the app to refresh progress.

Regent's phone conversation accepts text. Attachments and ordinary conversation actions such as moving, deleting or branching Regent are unavailable. Use the project conversation when you need those actions. The offline demo includes a Regent example with scripted replies.

New conversation opens an independent chat even while Regent is working or waiting for approval. Its existing work continues; return to Regent to follow up.

Completed replies keep the answer visible and fold the process under Worked for… (or Activity when timing is unavailable). Expand it to inspect the activity timeline, then open individual tools for their input, output and status. Running tools animate while the app is visible.
